# Adapted Architecture

**Open-use descriptor. Released April 21, 2026. Intentionally not trademarked.**

---

## What It Is

Adapted Architecture describes the structure an adaptive system builds from what it actually has — not from an ideal blueprint, but from its own history, its own constraints, and its own interactions with its environment.

The system adapts. The architecture reflects that adaptation. When pressure comes, the architecture either holds or it doesn't.

This applies to:
- Athletes rebuilding after injury
- Neurotrauma clients rebuilding after brain injury
- Students with learning differences building around what their system actually does
- AI systems maintaining identity and function under adversarial or degrading conditions
- Any adaptive system under load

---

## Why It's Not Trademarked

Adapted Architecture is intentionally available. It is a descriptor, not a product. Anyone working with adaptive systems — rehab clinicians, coaches, teachers, IEP teams, AI safety practitioners, researchers — can use it.

The underlying methodology (how Adapted Architecture is built, mapped, and accessed) is Neuroformation™, which is protected. The outcome frame is open.

## Where the Idea Already Existed

The term arrived April 21, 2026, but the observation behind it did not — six unconnected fields had already named pieces of the same idea, with no field aware of the others:

| Field | Their name for it | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Disability studies | Present-state functionality | Hutcheon & Wolbring, *M/C Journal*, 2013 |
| Neuroconstructivism | Different cognitive phenotype | Karmiloff-Smith, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 1998 |
| Rehabilitation science | Compensation | Levin, Kleim & Wolf, *Neurorehabilitation and Neural Repair*, 2009 |
| Ecological systems | Transformability | Holling, *Annual Review of Ecology and Systematics*, 1973 |
| Adaptive sport science | Constraints-led optimization | Davids et al., *Dynamics of Skill Acquisition*, 2008 |
| AI identity research | Self-evidencing | Friston, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 2010 |

Each field had the observation. None had bridged it to the others, and none had a name that traveled outside its own literature. Adapted Architecture is the bridge — checked against the existing research and found unclaimed as a term before it was used.
