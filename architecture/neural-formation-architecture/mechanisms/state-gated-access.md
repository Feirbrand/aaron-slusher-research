# State-Gated Access

**Status:** Locked
**NFA Layer:** Five-Layer Substrate / L1–L2
**Mechanism ID:** MECH-3

## What it explains

How NFA models the relationship between acute physiological state and the availability of some cognitive and emotional response routes.

## Definition

NFA proposes that current autonomic and physiological state can constrain access to some cognitive and emotional response routes. A skill, knowledge base, or intention may remain present while being less accessible under acute stress. The model does not claim that every route closes uniformly or that one marker predicts every individual's response.

## Origin and provenance

State-Gated Access is a practitioner-derived NFA distinction between the interpretation of a signal and the changing access to responses after stress or load escalates. It is informed by stress and executive-function research but is not presented as a complete scientific account of cognition under pressure.

## Evidence base

- Klinger (2001, NIJ-funded final report, *Police Responses to Officer-Involved Shootings*) interviewed officers about 113 reported shootings. Participants reported perceptual distortions during incidents, including diminished sound, tunnel vision, and altered time perception. This selected retrospective sample is ecologically salient descriptive evidence, not a general-population prevalence estimate.
- Schoofs, Preuß, and Wolf (2008, *Psychoneuroendocrinology*, DOI 10.1016/j.psyneuen.2008.02.004) experimentally induced psychosocial stress and found poorer working-memory performance in their sample.
- Qin et al. (2009, *Biological Psychiatry*, DOI 10.1016/j.biopsych.2009.03.006) found reduced working-memory-related dorsolateral prefrontal-cortex activity after experimentally induced acute stress in healthy women.
- Arnsten (2015, *Nature Neuroscience*, DOI 10.1038/nn.4087) reviews evidence that acute uncontrollable stress can reduce prefrontal cognitive control and shift behavior toward more reflexive circuitry.

## Evidence limits

Together, these sources provide convergent multi-level evidence that acute stress is associated with perceptual changes, working-memory impairment, and altered prefrontal executive-network activity. They inform, but do not independently validate, the NFA State-Gated Access model. They do not distinguish state effects from every possible individual difference.

## Claim ceiling

**Warranted at the evidence level:** acute stress is associated with changes in perception, working-memory performance, and prefrontal executive-network activity.

**NFA interpretation:** these changes can be usefully modeled as state-dependent constraints on access to some cognitive and emotional response routes.

**Not established:** precise prediction of which route changes first for an individual, a validated unified formula combining physiological indicators, or a validated multivariable readiness package.

## Boundary conditions

The model applies under genuine acute or accumulating physiological load. It does not claim that all responses change uniformly, and it does not establish that response availability is explained by state rather than trait in every context.

## How it appears in practice

**Acute stress:** the Klinger report describes auditory and visual changes reported during high-stakes incidents.

**Readiness and recovery:** individualized monitoring is an application area that requires separate validation from the mechanism claim.

## Relationship map

**Within the NFA model:** Signal-to-Constraint Conversion concerns how a signal may be interpreted before a stress response. Witness / Metacognitive Regulation concerns how automaticity may influence whether state-dependent performance changes disrupt execution.
