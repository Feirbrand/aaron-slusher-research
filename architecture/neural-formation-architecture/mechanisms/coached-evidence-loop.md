# Coached Evidence Loop

**Status:** Locked
**NFA Layer:** Routing Engine
**Mechanism ID:** MECH-2

## What it explains

How embodied proof updates identity faster than abstract belief alone.

## Definition

Telling someone they're capable rarely changes what they believe about themselves as fast or as durably as having them actually do the thing and register that they did it. The loop is: attempt → embodied evidence of outcome → identity update → next attempt calibrated to the update. Abstract reassurance skips the embodied-evidence step and produces a weaker, less durable update.

## Origin and provenance

Observed directly in coaching — verbal reassurance ("you can do this") reliably producing less durable change than structuring a task the person can actually complete and witness themselves completing. Named as a distinct mechanism from Signal-to-Constraint Conversion and Witness — this is specifically about how identity updates, not how signals convert or how attention holds.

## Evidence base

- Bandura, Adams & Beyer (1977, *J. Pers. Soc. Psychol.*, DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.35.3.125) — mastery-based treatment produced stronger and more generalized self-efficacy expectations than treatment based on vicarious experience alone.
- Model-based meta-analysis (N=8,965, DOI 10.1037/cou0000219) — performance accomplishments had by far the strongest unique association with self-efficacy, compared to other sources of efficacy information.
- Talsma et al. (2018, DOI 10.1016/j.lindif.2017.11.015) — reciprocal relationship: performance and self-efficacy update each other over time, not a one-way effect.

**Boundary, stated explicitly rather than blurred:** this evidence base supports mastery/performance experience updating self-efficacy and capability beliefs — a well-established, specific finding. It does not establish that embodied proof updates *identity* more broadly. That broader claim remains Aaron's practitioner interpretation, kept separate below, not scientific fact until identity-change literature is separately sourced.

## Claim ceiling

**Science-supported:** mastery/performance experiences strongly and specifically influence self-efficacy and capability beliefs — well-replicated.

**Aaron's practitioner observation, kept explicitly separate:** repeated embodied evidence can eventually alter what someone believes about who they are or can be — identity-level, not yet independently sourced. The distinction matters: self-efficacy research does not automatically become identity research just because the two are related in practice.

## Boundary conditions

Applies where a person can attempt and complete a concrete, self-verifiable task. Does not apply where no such task exists or where the gap between current and desired identity can't be bridged by any single achievable attempt.
