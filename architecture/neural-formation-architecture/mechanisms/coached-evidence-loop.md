# Coached Evidence Loop

**Status:** Locked
**NFA Layer:** Routing Engine
**Mechanism ID:** MECH-2

## What it explains

How performance evidence can update self-efficacy and, in the NFA interpretation, contribute to identity revision.

## Definition

Coached Evidence Loop is a practitioner-derived NFA model: attempt → embodied evidence of outcome → self-efficacy update → next attempt calibrated to that update. It distinguishes evidence generated through completing a concrete task from abstract reassurance about capability.

## Origin and provenance

The mechanism was developed as an NFA distinction between how signals are classified, how attention is regulated, and how performance evidence may update a person's capability beliefs. Its identity-level extension is a practitioner interpretation, not an independently established result.

## Evidence base

- Bandura, Adams, and Beyer (1977, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.35.3.125) found that mastery-based treatment produced stronger and more generalized self-efficacy expectations than treatment based on vicarious experience alone.
- Byars-Winston, Diestelmann, Savoy, and Hoyt (2017, *Journal of Counseling Psychology*, DOI 10.1037/cou0000219) conducted a model-based meta-analysis of 61 academic-self-efficacy studies (N=8,965). Performance accomplishments had the strongest unique association with self-efficacy after accounting for other sources.
- Talsma et al. (2018, *Learning and Individual Differences*, DOI 10.1016/j.lindif.2017.11.015) analyzed 11 longitudinal academic studies (N=2,688). Academic performance predicted later academic self-efficacy; reciprocal effects were found in adults rather than children.

## Evidence limits

The cited literature supports claims about self-efficacy in defined study contexts. It does not directly test a general comparison between coaching-based task completion and verbal reassurance, the relative durability of those approaches, or identity-level change.

## Claim ceiling

**Science-supported:** mastery and performance experiences can influence self-efficacy and capability beliefs.

**NFA interpretation:** repeated embodied evidence may contribute to identity revision. This identity-level extension is not presented as independently established evidence.

## Boundary conditions

The model is most applicable where a person can attempt and evaluate a concrete, self-verifiable task. It does not imply that every desired identity change can be bridged by one achievable attempt, or that task completion alone resolves broader social, clinical, or structural constraints.

## How it appears in practice

The chapter presents no client or session information. Its application to coached task design remains a practitioner interpretation that should be evaluated against defined outcomes.

## Relationship map

**Signal-to-Constraint Conversion** — the two mechanisms distinguish constraint classification from subsequent updating of capability beliefs.

**Habit Route Reinforcement** — repeated evidence-generating attempts may provide one route through which a response becomes more automatic within the NFA model.
